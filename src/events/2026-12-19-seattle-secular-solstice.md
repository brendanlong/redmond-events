The rationalist community's big winter gathering is back: **Seattle Secular Solstice** takes over Central Lutheran Church on Capitol Hill on **Saturday, December 19**. Doors and a vegan dinner start at **5 p.m.**, and the ceremony itself runs roughly **6 to 9 p.m.**

If you haven't been, Secular Solstice is a sort of concert-meets-ritual built around the longest night of the year. The evening deliberately moves from light into darkness and back: group singalongs and performed songs, readings that swing from sobering to rousing, and a moment of full darkness and silence in the middle before the candles come back on. This year's framing leans into how fast the future seems to be arriving — AI included — which will land with anyone who follows AI-safety conversations. No prior Solstice experience needed; it's built to work for first-timers.

This is the one event on the rationalist calendar that's worth the drive into Seattle even for Eastsiders who skip the weekly meetups. Central Lutheran is on 11th Ave at E Olive St, about a 25–30 minute drive from downtown Redmond via SR 520 on a Saturday evening; street parking on Capitol Hill is tight, so allow extra time to circle. Capitol Hill light rail station is about a 10-minute walk away if you'd rather ride in. There's an afterparty from 9:30 p.m. at a rationalist group house in Northgate for anyone who wants to keep going.

Tickets are $20 standard, with $40 supporter and $100 patron tiers that subsidize others, plus free "accessible" tickets for anyone for whom the price is a barrier — no questions asked. Get them at [seasolstice.org](https://seasolstice.org/).
