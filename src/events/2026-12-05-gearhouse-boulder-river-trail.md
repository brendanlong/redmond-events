The **Boulder River Trail**, off SR 530 west of Darrington, is one of the best low-elevation winter hikes in the North Cascades foothills. It starts on an old logging road, enters the Boulder River Wilderness, and follows a mossy canyon where several waterfalls pour down the far wall. On **Saturday, December 5**, Gearhouse is hiking it at a social pace. The group stops at the falls around mile 1.2, keeps going to a lunch turnaround at about 12:15 p.m., and then heads back before the early December dark sets in.

The full trail is 8.6 miles round trip with about 1,000 feet of gain. The group is doing roughly 4.5 hours of it, not the whole thing, and turns around before the stretch with broken bridges and deep mud. In early December, expect rock, roots, mud and a few small creek crossings. Bring rain pants as well as a jacket, waterproof boots, lunch (there's nowhere to buy food on the way), and a backup headlamp.

The time above is the **9:30 a.m. trailhead meet**. The Gearhouse van leaves Capitol Hill at 8:00 a.m., but from Redmond you're better off driving north on your own. Take SR 522 to I-5, then through Arlington and out SR 530. It's about **an hour and 20 minutes**, ending with 3.7 potholed miles of French Creek Road (Forest Road 2010) near milepost 41. The lot holds only about 15 cars, so carpooling helps, and no pass is required. Plan to be back at the car around 2 p.m. That leaves time to get home for the **Redmond Lights kick-off** at Downtown Park, which runs 4–8 p.m. the same evening.

It runs rain or shine with a five-member minimum (confirmed by Thursday, Dec. 3). RSVP on the [Gearhouse calendar](https://www.joingearhouse.com/).
