Gearhouse is running a Monday-night hike with a twist: an easy walk up **Denny Creek to the "waterslide"** at Snoqualmie Pass, done entirely **by headlamp**. The waterslide is a stretch of smooth granite slabs where the creek runs over the rock in wide sheets. It's a classic summer swimming spot, and on a November evening it will be dark and quiet, with no crowds. The organizers bring hot cocoa for the break at the slabs, and on a clear night the stars over the pass are a real treat.

The hike itself is gentle, about **2.5 to 3 miles round trip with 400 to 500 feet of climbing**. It's billed as a "social stroll," with regrouping at every junction and the pace set by the slowest hiker. The group stays on the main trail and turns around at the waterslide. There are no creek crossings in the dark, and nobody walks out onto the slick rock. This is a good, low-stakes first night hike.

The time above is the **trailhead start, around 5:50 p.m.** The Gearhouse van leaves the Capitol Hill café at 4:45 p.m., but from Redmond it makes far more sense to drive straight out I-90. The Denny Creek lot at exit 47 is about **50 minutes from downtown Redmond**, and the drive goes against Seattle's rush-hour traffic. Plan to be back at your car around 7:30 p.m. and home well before 9. Parking needs a Northwest Forest Pass or America the Beautiful pass, or you can pay at the lot.

You'll need a headlamp with fresh batteries, warm layers (the pass gets cold quickly after dark), a rain jacket, and shoes with good grip. Microspikes are worth packing if there's been early snow. It's free for members, and the event needs at least five sign-ups to run. RSVP on the [Gearhouse calendar](https://www.joingearhouse.com/).
