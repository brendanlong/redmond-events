Fall rain has the forests around here fruiting, and Gearhouse is running a two-part crash course in what's safe to pick. It's a good fit for the food-and-outdoors crowd, and a rare chance to learn mushroom ID from a guide instead of a sketchy phone app.

**Part 1, Friday, November 6, 6 p.m.** is a classroom-style presentation at the Gearhouse café on Capitol Hill: what grows in the Seattle region, how to identify it, how to pick responsibly, and how to tell a good find from a poisonous look-alike. No experience needed. **Part 2, Sunday, November 8, around 9 a.m.** takes the group into the woods on the Kanaskat-Palmer Loop with the instructor, checking every find together, then cooking the edible ones at the picnic area. Part 2 requires having attended Part 1, specifically because of the poisonous look-alikes, and it needs at least eight members to run.

Getting there: the Friday class is in Seattle on a weeknight, which is the usual Capitol Hill hassle, but it's a short evening and the payoff is the Sunday outing. Kanaskat-Palmer is near Ravensdale in southeast King County, roughly **a 45-minute drive** from Redmond; the 9 a.m. time is when the group meets at the trail, so driving straight there beats going into the city first. RSVP for both on the [Gearhouse calendar](https://www.joingearhouse.com/).
