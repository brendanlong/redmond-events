On **Sunday, November 22**, Gearhouse is taking a full day on **Bainbridge Island**: a morning at **Bloedel Reserve**, a group lunch in Winslow, and an afternoon hike to **Gazzam Lake**. It's a good fit for a wet late-autumn Sunday. Bloedel, about 150 acres of gardens and forest on the island's north end, is at its moodiest in the mist. It has a Japanese garden, meadows, ponds, about two miles of easy paths, and a moss garden that looks best in drizzle.

After lunch the group walks through second-growth forest from the Marshall Road trailhead to Gazzam Lake, a small lake popular with birds. A side trail then drops to a rocky beach on Puget Sound. That adds up to about **2.5 to 3 miles and up to 500 feet of climbing**, most of it on the stairs and switchbacks back up from the beach. You can wait at the lake if you'd rather skip that stretch. If the weather is truly nasty, the backup is a flat waterfront walk at Fort Ward Park, past old gun batteries and often within earshot of sea lions.

**Getting there from Redmond:** the Gearhouse van leaves Capitol Hill at 8:45 a.m. for the ferry, but you can join at **Bloedel Reserve at 10:30 a.m.** (the time listed above). On a Sunday morning it's about 30 minutes from Redmond to the Seattle ferry terminal, plus the 35-minute crossing. Walking on and catching Kitsap Transit's BI Ride from the Bainbridge terminal ($2) avoids the car-ferry line and the parking hunt. The group catches the ferry back around 4:10 p.m.

Plan on **roughly $60–70** for the day: about $13 as your share of the ferry fare, $22 for Bloedel admission, plus lunch. (Emmy's Vege House in Winslow is the vegetarian pick among the organizers' suggestions.) Bring a rain jacket, layers, and shoes that can handle mud and stairs. It runs rain or shine with a five-member minimum. RSVP on the [Gearhouse calendar](https://www.joingearhouse.com/).
