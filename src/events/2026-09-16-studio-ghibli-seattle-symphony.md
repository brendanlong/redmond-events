For anyone who grew up on Miyazaki, this is the show: the **Seattle Symphony performs the music of Studio Ghibli**, live, at newly-reopened Benaroya Hall on **Wednesday, September 16**. The program is built entirely from **Joe Hisaishi's** scores — the composer behind essentially every Ghibli soundtrack — including selections from *Howl's Moving Castle*, *My Neighbor Totoro*, *Kiki's Delivery Service*, and *Spirited Away*. Local musician Tomo Nakayama narrates an "Orchestra Stories" segment built around *My Neighbor Totoro*, so it's not just a concert — there's storytelling woven through it.

This lands squarely in nerdy-fandom territory: it's the kind of orchestral-meets-pop-culture crossover show (think Jonathan Coulton or a video-game symphony) that turns a night of "classical music" into something a Ghibli fan will actually get excited about. It's family-friendly, too — a good one if you want to introduce a kid to a live orchestra without a program full of unfamiliar composers.

Benaroya Hall is downtown, about a **30-minute drive from Redmond** — arena-adjacent traffic and parking apply, but a weeknight evening concert is an easier lift than a big arena show. Tickets are reportedly moving fast.

Tickets at [seattlesymphony.org](https://www.seattlesymphony.org/benaroyahall).
