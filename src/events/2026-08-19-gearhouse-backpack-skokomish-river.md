For the "let's actually go camp somewhere" crowd: **Gearhouse** is running a beginner-friendly overnight backpacking trip to the **Lower South Fork Skokomish River** on the Olympic Peninsula, Wednesday-Thursday, **August 19-20**. The trail is one of the gentler ways to get an overnight in your legs — 8.6 to 10.3 miles round-trip (depending on where you turn around) with under 1,000 feet of gain, weaving through groves of 500-year-old old-growth Douglas firs alongside the roaring South Fork. Group size is capped at six people, so this is a small, easygoing trip rather than a big production.

**The time listed above is when the group reaches the trailhead**, not when the carpool leaves the city. Gearhouse's Summit-tier shuttle departs Capitol Hill at 8 a.m. and arrives around **10:30 a.m.**, with hiking starting around 11. Only three Summit spots get the shuttle; the other three (general-member) spots need to arrange their own ride to the trailhead — which, done directly from Redmond rather than via Seattle, is roughly a **2-hour drive** out to the Skokomish Valley near Shelton. Everyone camps along the river Wednesday night (Homestead Camp is one likely spot) and hikes back out Thursday morning, aiming to be back at the cars by lunchtime.

This is short notice — the trip leaves in three days — so if an easy overnight in old-growth forest sounds appealing, RSVP soon; Gearhouse charges a $50 no-show fee if you bail without at least a day's notice, so only commit if the dates work.

RSVP on the [Gearhouse calendar](https://www.joingearhouse.com/). Bring the 10 essentials plus your overnight kit — tent, sleeping bag, pad, and a water filter, and coordinate shared gear with the group.
